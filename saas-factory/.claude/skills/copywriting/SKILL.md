# Copywriting

Last reviewed: 2026-10.

## Principles
- Voice of customer first: mine interviews for pains, outcomes and exact phrases.
- Match the reader's awareness level (unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware,
  product-aware, most aware). Cold traffic needs the problem first.
- One page, one goal, one primary CTA.
- Benefits are outcomes for the reader; features are proof.
- Specific beats clever. Clarity beats creativity.

## Frameworks (pick one per piece)
- PAS: Problem, Agitate, Solution. Good for pain-led pages and emails.
- AIDA: Attention, Interest, Desire, Action.
- Before-After-Bridge.

## Rules
1. Headline states the outcome in customer words; subhead says who and how.
2. CTA says what happens next ("Start your 14-day trial"), not "Submit".
3. Each benefit has proof: a number, a screenshot, a quote.
4. Address objections from interviews in the copy or the FAQ.
5. Microcopy: errors say what happened and how to fix it; no blame.
6. Scannable: short paragraphs, meaningful subheads, front-loaded sentences.
7. No false urgency or dark patterns (also illegal under EU consumer law).

## Audit checklist
- [ ] Could a stranger say what this is, for whom, and why it is better in 5 seconds?
- [ ] One primary CTA per page or email
- [ ] Every benefit backed by proof; no unverifiable superlatives
- [ ] Objections from research answered
- [ ] Passes `human-writing` audit

## Sources

- Joanna Wiebe / Copyhackers, voice-of-customer research method
- Nielsen Norman Group, web writing and microcopy research
