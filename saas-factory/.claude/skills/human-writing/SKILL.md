# Human writing

Last reviewed: 2026-10. Founder overrides: `_config/writing-style.md`.

## Principles
- Write for one specific reader with one specific problem.
- Concrete beats abstract: numbers, names, examples, the customer's own words.
- Say it once, plainly. Cut anything the reader would not miss.
- Idiomatic US English: contractions are fine, phrasal verbs are fine,
  sentence length varies naturally.

## Banned patterns (AI tells)
- Words: delve, tapestry, testament, realm, landscape, seamless, robust,
  leverage (as a verb), elevate, unlock, empower, game-changer, cutting-edge,
  revolutionize, embark, navigate (figurative), crucial, pivotal, foster, vibrant.
- Openers/closers: "In today's fast-paced world", "Let's dive in",
  "In conclusion", "Whether you're X or Y", "Ready to take X to the next level?"
- Structures: "It's not just X, it's Y"; "not only... but also" as a reflex;
  lists that always come in threes; rhetorical question then answer;
  em-dash in every paragraph; colon-heavy headlines ("X: The Ultimate Guide").
- Vague praise or hedging: "very", "truly", "incredibly", "arguably", "it's worth noting".
- Emoji as decoration, bold on random phrases, title case everywhere.
- Fake specificity: invented stats, unnamed "studies show", generic testimonials.

## Rules
1. Draft, then cut 20%.
2. Replace every abstract claim with a fact, an example or delete it.
3. Read it aloud mentally: if a friend would not say it, rewrite it.
4. Vary rhythm: mix short and long sentences.
5. Sentence case for headings and buttons.
6. Use customer vocabulary from interviews (`synthesis.md`).

## Audit checklist
- [ ] Zero banned words and structures (search the text for them)
- [ ] Every claim is specific and true; no invented numbers
- [ ] No sentence could be pasted into a competitor's site unchanged
- [ ] Reads naturally aloud; contractions where a person would use them
- [ ] Matches `_config/brand-voice.md`
