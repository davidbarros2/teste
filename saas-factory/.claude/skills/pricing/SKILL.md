# Pricing

Last reviewed: 2026-10.

## Rules
1. Price on value, not cost. Anchor on the cost of the problem and alternatives.
2. Choose a value metric that grows with customer value (seats, usage,
   locations, projects). Avoid metrics customers cannot predict.
3. Few plans (1-3), clearly differentiated by who they are for.
4. Annual plans with a meaningful discount (often ~2 months free).
5. Trial: card-required trials convert better but reduce sign-ups; freemium
   only when free users create value (virality) and support costs stay low.
6. B2B niche micro SaaS usually underprices: test higher before lower.
7. Grandfather existing customers on price increases, or give clear notice.
8. Show prices consistently with tax treatment (B2C incl. VAT in the EU).

## Testing
- Willingness-to-pay questions in interviews (Van Westendorp style ranges).
- Pre-sales and smoke tests at a real price.
- Change one variable at a time; measure conversion and churn, not only sign-ups.

## Audit checklist
- [ ] Value metric explained in one sentence
- [ ] Each plan has a clear target customer
- [ ] Price justified by evidence (interviews, alternatives' cost)
- [ ] Tax display matches legal-framework

## Sources
- Madhavan Ramanujam & Georg Tacke, *Monetizing Innovation*
- Peter van Westendorp, Price Sensitivity Meter (1976)
- EU Price Indication Directive 98/6/EC and consumer rules (B2C)
